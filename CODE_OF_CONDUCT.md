# Code of conduct

Patch is a small project and we want it to stay a friendly place to work on. This is a short version of the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/), version 2.1, which applies in full to issues, pull requests, discussions and every other space of this repository.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation a harassment-free experience for everyone, whatever their age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience, nationality, appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient. Assume good intent, and say it plainly when something is unclear.
- Give and accept constructive feedback. Critique the code, not the person.
- Take responsibility for mistakes, apologize, and learn from them.
- Keep the focus on what is best for the project and its users.

## What is not acceptable

- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Trolling, insults, personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing other people's private information, such as an address or email, without permission.
- Anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Enforcement

The maintainer is responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards, and may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, issues and other contributions that do not follow them, or block a person for behavior that is inappropriate, threatening or harmful.

To report a problem, use GitHub's private reporting on this repository: **Security → Report a vulnerability**, and say in the first line that it is a conduct report rather than a security issue. You can also report abusive content to GitHub directly. Reports are handled privately and in good faith, and the person who reported is not named without their consent.

Consequences range from a private warning to a temporary or permanent ban, depending on the impact.
